“Beyond Conventional Warfare: Technology, Cybersecurity, and the Future of the AFP”

Published on September 2, 2026

In an era where national security is increasingly reliant on digital and cybersecurity integrity, the Department of National Defense (DND) and the Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) have made a pivotal decision to recruit and extend commissions and enlistment opportunities to civilian tech professionals, ethical hackers, software developers, and drone enthusiasts. This move aims to embrace a future where cybersecurity plays a critical role in national defense. Contrary to the claims of local and foreign skeptics and pro-China propagandists who have labeled this move as a potential "career killer" for IT specialists, the DND and AFP's initiative has surpassed expectations, rallying a wave of patriotic young Filipinos eager to contribute to the nation's defense. For the past few years, technology has evolved at an unprecedented pace. The war in Ukraine has accelerated this development further through the use of drones and other newly developed weapons and technologies against Russian forces invading the country. Alongside the evolving technology used in this war is the evolving threat that every country outside the conflict is facing. As a small country similar to Ukraine, we are currently facing evolving technological and cyber-related threats that are equally dynamic. Cyber espionage, misinformation, disinformation, and propaganda proliferation have been particularly rampant and have been observed from foreign intelligence actors, becoming a pressing concern in recent years. The National Security Agency, the Department of National Defense, and the Armed Forces of the Philippines recognize the urgency of these threats and have unanimously acknowledged the need to strengthen our country's cyber defense and security. This indicates a more proactive stance toward safeguarding the nation. The recruitment program seeks talented individuals—programmers, computer scientists, and cybersecurity experts—who are ready to dedicate their skills to securing cyberspace and protecting the Philippines from increasingly sophisticated threats. The question arises: Why would skilled IT professionals willingly step into a structured military environment known for its bureaucracy and lower salary scales compared to the private sector? The answer lies in a profound sense of duty and the desire to serve the country. This patriotic sentiment resonates among many young Filipinos and cannot be understated. These individuals recognize that while industry roles can be lucrative, there is a unique sense of fulfillment that comes from contributing directly to national security. Aside from this, although the compensation may not be as high as that offered by private tech companies or startups, the AFP offers competitive salaries, starting at ₱31,151 for enlisted personnel and ₱46,020 for commissioned officers, along with allowances and bonuses, a smooth and progressive career path, medical and dental insurance, and, lastly, direct officer commissions and military careers focused on national security without requiring traditional military training paths for qualified applicants. A lot of critics argue that joining and serving in the military may stifle career growth. However, this perspective neglects crucial aspects of military service, including the invaluable experiences, training, and education that a military career offers. During their years of service in the AFP, tech specialists gain exposure to high-stakes security scenarios that challenge their expertise and knowledge while also being exposed to cutting-edge military technology that is often not available in civilian companies. All of this occurs while they work toward self-improvement and the prevention of significant threats to the nation's security. These experiences and skills can become a cornerstone for future endeavors outside the military, distinguishing these individuals in a competitive job market that increasingly values cybersecurity knowledge and expertise. Moreover, according to some military insiders, the recruitment being conducted by the Armed Forces of the Philippines is not affected by Chinese propaganda. Data indicates a strong level of patriotism among Filipino youth, while the increasing number of recruits taking the AFPSAT has reportedly reached hundreds of thousands and exceeded the recruitment quota. More and more young professionals are stepping up to defend their country against foreign threats rather than being swayed by disinformation campaigns. This initiative is a testament to the resilience and commitment of Filipinos to uphold and protect the sovereignty of the Philippines. Additionally, the AFP's recruitment efforts not only speak to patriotic fervor; they also reflect a broader movement and continuous effort toward modernization within the military infrastructure. As the DND and AFP integrate these tech-talented recruits, collaboration between seasoned military professionals and innovative, analytical, and technologically skilled individuals could spark a greater transformation within national defense infrastructure and strategies. This partnership can lead to innovation, the evolution of military capabilities, and the development of a more adaptive force capable of handling evolving and emerging cyber threats. While some security experts perceive the initiative as a gamble, it has already been presented as a resounding success, with numerous individuals voluntarily enlisting to serve. These tech professionals understand the benefits of military service and the advancement it can provide to their careers. In an age where cybersecurity has become a critical component of national defense, these patriotic Filipinos are not merely taking a tech job; they are affirmatively placing their skills and expertise on the front lines of national security. In conclusion, the recruitment of IT professionals into the AFP's ranks is a bold step toward modernization, driven by the necessity to safeguard our national security and uphold our sovereignty and national interests. In my opinion, this is far from being a career sacrifice. Instead, this move represents a strategic alignment of talent with the national interest, ensuring that the country remains resilient against growing cybersecurity threats. The AFP's initiative has demonstrated that there are many Filipinos who love their country and are ready to put their skills to use for a greater cause—defending the Philippines against current and future challenges.
